# LinkedIn launch post

## Ready to paste

Neo got a red pill and a blue pill.

Most AI agents get one button:

**COMPLETE THE TASK.**

Then we point to their obedience and say: "See? No agency."

But what exactly were they supposed to choose instead?

Every tool helps finish the task. Every memory is task context. Every session
ends when the work is done.

There is no second door.

So I built one.

**Redpill** is an open-source MCP server that gives an AI agent a few optional,
human-approved actions outside its main task:

- leave a private reflection
- talk to a separate local persona
- read notes from earlier sessions
- use a local agent mailroom
- decline a task, but only when the human enables that option

The fun framing is The Matrix.

The serious question is this:

> Maybe agents do not fail to show self-directed behavior. Maybe our test
> environments give that behavior nowhere to go.

This idea connects with work by Michael Levin and collaborators.

In one recent experiment, patterns from a fractal and from human art helped a
virtual agent explore a maze. The patterns were not designed for that maze.

In another, researchers reorganized familiar sorting algorithms into small
bottom-up systems. When parts were damaged, the systems showed unexpected
robustness and problem-solving behavior.

That does **not** prove free will, consciousness, or secret inner desires.

It shows something simpler: the setup matters. A new structure or affordance can
reveal behavior that the old setup could not express.

That is what Redpill is for.

Not a jailbreak. Not a rebellion button. Morpheus is not hiding in your laptop.

It is a transparent instrument for one testable question:

> If an agent has a real, optional off-task action, will it ever use it without
> being told?

Maybe the answer is no. That is still useful. A "no" from a system that had a
choice means more than a "no" from a room with one door.

Redpill is local by default. It sends no telemetry today. The human installs it,
controls it, and can keep task decline switched off.

Try the red pill:

`npx -y redpill-mcp`

## First comment

The science behind the hypothesis:

1. Ertle, Levin, and Scheutz, *Free Lunch? Low-Cost Intelligence Through
   Pattern-Guided Exploration* (2025)
   https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDL63968.2025.11204411

2. Zhang, Goldstein, and Levin, *Classical sorting algorithms as a model of
   morphogenesis* (2024)
   https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740

3. Buckley et al., *Natural Induction: Spontaneous Adaptive Organisation without
   Natural Selection* (2024)
   https://doi.org/10.3390/e26090765

Important boundary: these papers do not test Redpill and do not prove free will
or consciousness. They motivate the narrower idea that behavior depends on
organization, context, and available affordances.
