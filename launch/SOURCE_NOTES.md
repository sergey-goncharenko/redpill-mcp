# Source notes and claim boundaries

Use this file when editing [POST.md](POST.md). It records which statements are
directly supported and which are Redpill hypotheses.

## Free Lunch? Low-Cost Intelligence Through Pattern-Guided Exploration

- Authors: Emily A. Ertle, Michael Levin, Matthias Scheutz
- Venue/year: IEEE ICDL, 2025
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDL63968.2025.11204411

Supported statements from the abstract:

- Patterns from a Halley fractal and human-generated art guided virtual-agent
  maze exploration better than patterns with less structure.
- Replacing half of a deep Q-learning agent's environmental input with
  arbitrary fractal information increased exploration ability.
- Static internal patterns from unrelated sources can combine with sensory
  information to produce more complex behavior.

Do not turn this into:

- proof that irrelevant information always improves agents
- proof of free will, consciousness, or intrinsic goals
- evidence about language-model agents specifically

## Classical sorting algorithms as a model of morphogenesis

- Authors: Taining Zhang, Adam Goldstein, Michael Levin
- Journal/year: Adaptive Behavior, 2024
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740
- Open preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05375

Supported statements from the abstract:

- Classical sorting algorithms were reorganized as bottom-up arrays of elements
  with minimal local agency and tested with damaged elements.
- The reorganized arrays sorted more reliably and robustly than traditional
  implementations in the presence of errors.
- Reported behavior included temporarily reducing progress to navigate a defect
  and unexpected clustering in mixed-algorithm arrays.

Do not turn this into:

- proof that the entire experimental dynamics were deterministic
- proof that the elements possessed phenomenal experience or free will
- proof that all simple algorithms contain latent intelligence

## Natural Induction: Spontaneous Adaptive Organisation without Natural Selection

- Authors: Christopher L. Buckley, Tim Lewens, Michael Levin, Beren Millidge,
  Alexander Tschantz, Richard A. Watson
- Journal/year: Entropy, 2024
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/e26090765

Supported statements from the abstract:

- Networks of viscoelastic connections were subjected to repeated disturbances
  and relaxation.
- Interaction between physical optimization and slow structural accommodation
  produced improving problem-solving competency with experience.
- The reported adaptation did not require supervised training or a system-level
  reward.

Do not turn this into:

- adaptation without any mechanism or prior structure
- a demonstration in LLMs
- proof of consciousness or metaphysical freedom

## Redpill hypothesis

The cited papers do not test Redpill and do not establish its hypothesis. They
motivate a methodological stance:

> Observable competence depends on organization, context, and available
> affordances; absence of behavior in a setup that cannot express it is weak
> evidence about the underlying disposition.

Redpill tests whether optional, operator-sanctioned off-task affordances are ever
used by agents without explicit instruction, and whether continuity changes that
rate. A tool invocation is a behavior to explain, not proof of an inner state.

## Pre-post checklist

- [ ] Keep the first-person claim as a hypothesis, not a scientific conclusion.
- [ ] Include "not proof of free will/consciousness" near the main claim.
- [ ] Link primary sources, not only interviews or summaries.
- [ ] Do not imply Levin or coauthors endorse Redpill.
- [ ] Do not claim a null result proves absence of agency.
- [ ] Do not claim a non-zero invocation proves desire, selfhood, or sentience.
- [ ] State that Redpill is transparent, operator-installed, and local by default.
- [ ] State that the current release sends no telemetry.
